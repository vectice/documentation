# 🔠 Vectice Concepts

Vectice enables you to easily capture and share the keys assets for data science projects milestones that AI/ML teams produce such as datasets, code, notebooks, models, or runs. Then Vectice auto-generates documentation for those artifacts and enable you to seamlessly integrate them to your project templates at any phase of the lifecyle: from business requirements to production deployments. For managers, Vectice is the solution of choice for reviewing ongoing project progress, securing team knowledge, promoting collaboration and showcasing initiatives impact. &#x20;

<table><thead><tr><th width="211">Term</th><th>Definition</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Organization</td><td>An organization is a top-level concept in Vectice when a user first joins. Typically this resembles the real-world organization that you work in. You are always part of a single organization.<br>An organization has: <br>- At least one Org Admin who can invite/manage users and create workspaces.<br>- Users who are parts of the organization, can login into Vectice and access workspaces but don't have org admin priviledges.</td></tr><tr><td>Workspace</td><td><p>Workspaces are containers to organize projects, assets, and users.</p><p>Every user who joins Vectice has access to a personal workspace for their own usage and other workspaces to learn and collaborate with other users in the organization.<br>An organization will have many workspaces and only Org Admin can create a new workspace in the organization.<br>Workspaces have Admin and Members with different privileges.</p></td></tr><tr><td>Project</td><td>A project reflects a typical Machine Learning project, with its different phases, and the associated assets like code, datasets, models, and documentation. You can define multiple projects within each workspace. </td></tr><tr><td>Stages</td><td>The stages reflect the real-life stages or phases of the project lifecycle like Business Understanding, Data Preparation, Modeling, Deployment, etc. Stages let you document the goals, the assets, and the outcomes of the work along with the status. <br>Stages help data science teams and managers visually organize the project, enforce best practices, bring consistency, and capture knowledge. You can easily replicate your existing workflows or create new ones since they’re configurable</td></tr><tr><td>Datasets</td><td>Datasets reflect the datasets used for analysis, cleaning, model training, and validation. Datasets from various source systems can be registered in the catalog along with their metadata. Vectice will automatically version the metadata of datasets to enable lineage and comparison of versions.</td></tr><tr><td>Models</td><td>Models represent the machine learning models created and trained during the modeling process along with their hyper-parameters and the metrics from the training or production process. Models are versioned and tagged with their deployment environment within Vectice.</td></tr><tr><td>Runs</td><td>Runs are just code executions captured in Vectice, and they are important to reproduce work for existing projects. Each run can have one or more input assets, code references, and output assets. Runs are grouped into Jobs with a JobType and name, to easily identify them.</td></tr><tr><td>Code</td><td>Code and notebooks are attached to Runs with references to the underlying assets typically stored in a source control system like github or gitlab. Notebooks can also be directly uploaded in Stages to facilitate documentation of the project.</td></tr><tr><td>Contributor</td><td>Any workspace member who edits the project content or pushes any assets to a project through the Vectice APIs, becomes a project Contributor.</td></tr></tbody></table>
